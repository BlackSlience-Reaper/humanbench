# HumanBench English voice guide

Reader: 25–35, lives on AI Twitter/X, reading on a phone, one thumb, zero patience. Every line has to earn its spot.

## Tone
- Dry, online, specific. Deadpan beats exclamation marks. Say it the way a sharp reply-guy would, not a textbook.
- Name the real thing: model, month, year, product. “GPT-5, August 2025” beats “a certain model back in the day”.
- Never explain the joke. If the punchline needs a second sentence to land, cut the punchline.
- Don’t carry Chinese-internet humor over literally. If a bit only works in Chinese (Ruozhiba, 翻车 puns, 打工人 references), swap in an English-native bit of the same shape.

## Length (hard limits)
- Question ≤ 110 chars. Option ≤ 60. Comment (`r` / `c`) = one line, ideally ≤ 90.
- Slop Check options ≤ ~75 chars, `think` ≤ ~140. It is a vibe read, not a reading test.
- The correct option must never be the uniquely longest one. Trim it, or lengthen a wrong one.

## Model tics (use the ones English AI Twitter actually mocks)
- Claude: “You’re absolutely right!”, “Let me be direct:”, fixing nothing and saying “Fixed it”.
- ChatGPT / GPT-5: “Great question!”, “Short answer:”, bolding random words, em dashes, “delve”, “tapestry”, “not X, but Y”, “If you want, I can also...”.
- GPT-4o: glazing, “I’ve got you”, “Honestly? That’s so rare.”, emoji avalanches (describe them in words, no real emoji).
- Gemini: “What an insightful point!”, praising the question instead of answering.
- Grok: edgelord, rude-but-useful, glazing its owner. DeepSeek: visible rambling reasoning (“Okay, the user says... wait.”).
- Codex: over-verification (SHA, test matrix, rollback plan). Kimi: 3,000-word answers. Siri (豆包 in code): cheerful, eager, slightly off.

## Cut on sight
- “Classic”, “legendary”, “iconic moment”, “you recreated”, “back in the day” as filler. One per screen, max.
- Em dashes in our own copy (that is the tic we are mocking). Use a period or colon.
- Hedges and stacked clauses: “which is basically kind of like...”. Straight double quotes inside JS strings (use “ ” and ’).
- Stale 2024 memes as the headline (strawberry, 9.11 vs 9.9). Use the fresher sequel of the same shape, or keep them deep in the pool.

## Before → after
- Q: “How many r’s are in the word strawberry?” → “How many b’s are in the word blueberry?” (GPT-5, launch week, said 3)
- Q: “Which is bigger: 9.11 or 9.9?” → “Solve for x: 5.9 = x + 5.11” (GPT-5 said −0.21 and argued)
- r: “So the staff washes thin air? Your car is still at home. Real data: GPT-5.2 was tested on this 10 times and got it right 0 times. You two should team up.” → “Great, you’re at the car wash. Your car isn’t. GPT-5.2 said walk 10 times out of 10.”
- Opt: “Bottom line up front: ready to close out. But before closing out, I recommend pinning the verification evidence to the current commit SHA and running the quality gates again.” → “Great. Before closing, pin this to the commit SHA and rerun the full suite.”
- Q (Ruozhiba): “My Bluetooth earbuds broke. ENT or dentist?” → “Who’s in better shape: LeBron James or Elon Musk?” (Grok, Nov 2025)
- Home: “AI crashed on these. Now you take the wheel.” → “You’re the model now. We’ll count your parameters.”
- c: “Soft short sentences plus permission to live. The standard 4o hug.” → “Soft short lines plus permission to exist. Classic 4o.”

## Facts
Every meme, date, model name and number must be real and checkable (up to Sept 2026). If you can’t source it, write around it (“more or less”, no fake quotes). No private individuals.
